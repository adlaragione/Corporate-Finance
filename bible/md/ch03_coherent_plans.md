# Chapter 3 — Special Topic 01: Coherent Plans

Source: Special Topic 01 (file L03.pdf, 16 slides). Session 5 of Part One. Based on Rossi & Giustinelli, "The Coherence Side of Rationality: Theory and Evidence from Firm Plans" (forthcoming RFS).

## Workflow
Today: Forecasts → Cash flows → Value.

## Why good-looking forecasts can still produce bad valuations
- Each assumption may look reasonable in isolation; the problem is whether they fit together.
- Errors come from inconsistency across revenue, margins, CapEx, WC, debt and terminal assumptions.
- **The unit of analysis is the forecast system, not a single forecast.**

## Coherence ≠ accuracy
| | Accurate ex post | Inaccurate ex post |
|---|---|---|
| Coherent ex ante | Good process, good outcome | Good process / bad luck / bad outcome |
| Incoherent ex ante | Bad process, lucky outcome | Bad process / bad outcome / predictable error |

**Accuracy is judged after realizations; coherence is assessed before realizations.** (Added) Incoherence produces predictable (systematic) errors.

## Rules of thumb (least → most coherent)
1. Plain growth — average of the 2–3 most recent annual growth rates of each item.
2. Percentage of sales — each item grows like sales.
3. Economies of scale — OLS of each item's growth on contemporaneous sales growth; intercept = fixed component; slope × forecast sales growth = variable component.
4. Industry-based — as 3 but only same-industry firms.
5. Disaggregated/multivariate — "kitchen sink" with all variables.

(Added) g_X = α + β g_Sales; % of sales is α = 0, β = 1. E.g., α = 1%, β = 0.6, g_Sales = 10% → g_X = 7% (margin expands).

## "Everything grows with sales" (all +10%)
Sales (demand, share, capacity support?); COGS/SG&A (fixed costs, scale economies?); NWC (WC intensity constant?); CapEx (maintenance CapEx?). **Percentage of sales is not wrong by definition; it is wrong when it ignores the economics of the business.**

## The paper's findings
1. CFO forecasts often align with rules of thumb.
2. Plain growth and % of sales most incoherent; multivariate most coherent; nearly half of CFO forecasts align with plain growth and % of sales.
3. Incoherence is associated with predictable forecast errors, lower performance, underinvestment.

## How incoherent plans distort valuation
Incoherent plan → wrong reinvestment → wrong FCFO → wrong terminal value → wrong EV and equity value.
- Sales growth without CapEx → FCFO overstated
- WC flat despite growth → cash needs understated
- Debt/cash as plug → financing hides operating inconsistency
- Terminal growth not reachable → terminal value becomes fantasy
- Margin expansion without mechanism → terminal margin overstated

(Added example) EBIT 100, t 40%, D&A 50, ΔWC 10: CapEx 50 → FCFO 50; coherent CapEx 80 → FCFO 20.

## JJ Jones: which assumptions must fit together?
Revenue +10%, COGS 60%, opex 10%; excess assets 0; required cash 5%; AR 50 days; inventory 80 days; net PPE/revenue 1.2; AP 40 days; accrued 20%; debt balancing. What else must be true for one coherent business? JJ Jones is a coherence exercise. (Added: constant margins = no scale economies; PPE/revenue 1.2 = capacity grows with sales; days fixed = WC grows with sales; debt funds growth deficits; 10% growth cannot last forever.)

## Coherence checklist
1. Story-to-driver; 2. Revenue-to-capacity; 3. Margin; 4. Working capital; 5. Financing (consistent with FCFE); 6. Terminal (reachable from the explicit forecast).

## Plausible ≠ coherent
Excel balances inconsistent models; AI generates plausible narratives → neither guarantees coherence. **A model that balances is not necessarily a business plan that makes sense.**

## Discussion question (solution our own)
Plan: sales +12%/yr; EBITDA margin 15% → 22%; CapEx < depreciation; NWC constant; debt repaid quickly; terminal growth 4%.
(i) Coherent if: post-investment spare capacity + operating leverage, or asset-light scalable model, plus improved cash cycle.
(ii) Red flags: CapEx < D&A with growth (capacity); flat NWC (WC); margin jump without mechanism (margin); fast deleveraging built on inflated FCF (financing); 4% perpetual growth with no reinvestment (terminal).

## Takeaways
Coherence is a property of the system; plausible line by line ≠ coherent overall; rules of thumb only if they respect economics; incoherence distorts FCFO, TV, leverage, value; test story, assumptions, statements and valuation together. "In valuation, discipline is not only getting the formula right. It is making the assumptions fit together."

## Formula sheet
Plain growth; % of sales (X/Sales constant); g_X = α + β g_Sales; g = ROIC × reinvestment rate.

## Exam traps
Coherence ex ante vs accuracy ex post; ranking of RoTs; nearly half of CFOs use the two least coherent RoTs; consequences (predictable errors, lower performance, underinvestment); intercept = fixed, slope = variable; % of sales not wrong per se; balancing ≠ coherent; six checks.
